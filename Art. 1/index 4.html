“Spoiled brat!”

A workplace for people with disabilities, a wage of 38 kr an hour, and one sentence that hurt.

By Alina Körber, Marit Duddeck and Tiril Langeland Goodchild

During one of the last conversations with a superior, at a learning apprentice’s workplace Anna was called a “spoiled brat”. She sat down, stressed a lot, and wanted to have a conversation with her manager about her wages. She had just finished being a learning candidate and was supposed to be working for them for a few more months. Anna had expectations to be paid more since she finally had done her education and found out they were still paying her the same: 38kr an hour.

She was devastated and broke down. The manager was trying to comfort her but used the wrong words for an employee. He said that Anna had improved a lot from the spoiled brat she was when she arrived. It hurt her. She herself said: “being referred to as a ‘spoiled brat’ was depressing and I struggled to wake up the next days.”

The workplace is a place supposed to be suited for people with disabilities. Anna has been diagnosed with both ADHD and Autism. She was a learning apprentice there for two years. She was paid like any of the employees they had there. The issue is she was producing more than most of the other employees who were hired there with disabilities. She was earning the exact same, and they never reevaluated her to see if she could become a proper apprentice. Most of their employees also were having disability on the side and this job was mostly something to have to do during their day. For Anna it was her only income and that it didn’t even cover her travel to and from work.

When she first got to the workplace, she got presented with an “incomplete” contract, containing a total of four sentences with information about her work and her wage. She was only 17 and never had a job before. She had no idea how a proper contract was supposed to look like and what it should contain.

§ § §

In Norway, several laws protect people with disabilities. The Equality and Anti-Discrimination Act (Likestillings- og diskrimineringsloven) prohibits discrimination and gives a right to reasonable accommodation at work. In specially adapted workplaces (Varig tilrettelagt arbeid, VTA), the Working Environment Act (Arbeidsmiljøloven) generally applies, including rules on contracts, working hours and safety. Wages must be transparent and unlawful deductions are not allowed, and the Guardianship Act (Vergemålsloven) can protect against financial exploitation.

Considering all that, it is safe to say that this place and work took her love for cooking and making pastries.
